## The kill-shot was run and it failed

Both rounds converged on one attack, and it was the right one to make. The
binomial p-value assumes signs agree by chance half the time. That is false
whenever both series lean the same way: if 90% of APRs and 90% of our figures
were positive, signs would coincide 82% of the time with no relationship at
all. Round 2 predicted the corrected p-value would "likely exceed 0.05".

Measured:

| sample | agree | fair-coin P | null rate from marginals | corrected P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all traded | 9/11 | 0.0327 | 0.5041 | **0.0348** |
| ≥20 fills | 9/9 | 0.0020 | 0.5062 | **0.0022** |

The correction moves the p-value by 0.002. Positive-APR share among the 11 is
55%, and among our own figures also 55%, so the product returns almost exactly
0.5. **The prediction was specific and it is refuted by measurement.**

`match_rate_under_independence()` and `binomial_tail_p()` are now in
`pool_validation.py` with tests, and both nulls print side by side, so this
cannot be quietly re-assumed later.

## B — CONFIRMED, with three corrections to the claim

Sign agreement 9/11 (corrected P = 0.0348), Spearman +0.52. On the ≥20-fill
subset, 9/9 and +0.72.

**Correction 1 — "140 active pools" was wrong, and I found it while writing
the blind verdict.** `status=1` was read as "active". It is not: all 11 pools
that actually trade carry `status=0`, including LLP with $76.6M of assets and
Edge & Hedge with $2.06M. The field does not separate on holdings either —
103 of 179 `status=0` pools report TVL above zero, as do 51 of 140 `status=1`.
**The meaning of `status` is not established and is recorded as unknown.**

**Correction 2 — the 9/9 figure is not a second, stronger result.** It is the
same 11 observations minus the two smallest, and those two are exactly the
disagreements. The `fills>=20` threshold predates this run and is used
throughout the track, but quoting the improved p-value after seeing which
pools disagreed is close to double-counting. Both rounds of the falsifier said
so independently. **The honest headline is 9/11, P = 0.035.**

**Correction 3 — the sample is selected and the claim did not say so.** Eleven
of 319 pools — 3% — appear in our tape, and they are the large end: LLP holds
$76.6M, Edge & Hedge $2.06M, while the smallest visible hold ~$900. The
defensible statement is that the reconstruction agrees **for large, actively
trading pools**, not "for pools".

## Stronger than the claim asserted: magnitudes line up too

Found while writing the blind verdict, before unsealing. The claim restricted
itself to sign and order because APR and a 5-day gross figure are not
commensurable. But normalising our PnL by the published `total_asset_value` —
a field the reconstruction never touches — puts them on comparable footing:

| pool | our 5d return on TVL | published APR |
|---|---|---|
| LLP | +0.029% | +11.04% |
| Edge & Hedge | +0.328% | +12.73% |
| Syzygy | +7.859% | +56.50% |
| DSC - L | +5.862% | +322.66% |
| PHP Trading Desk | −1.875% | −7.72% |
| Alleged Alpha | −0.395% | −12.07% |

These do not match numerically and should not: five days at +0.029%
annualises near +2% against a published 11%. But the sign and the order of
magnitude track across four decades of pool size, which sign agreement alone
would not show.

## Open, and named rather than buried

- **Independence of the 11 is assumed, not established.** Round 2 raised this
  and neither round-1 verdict caught it: if these pools are mostly makers in
  one market regime, they are not 11 independent trials and the effective
  sample is smaller. Untested.
- **No p-value for Spearman.** +0.52 over 11 points has no null distribution
  attached. Both verdicts missed this in round 1.
- **n = 11 is one experiment.** The project's own precedent: range bars run
  five times on one unvalidated scale counted as one experiment, not five.
  Re-run as days accumulate, with the criterion written first.
- **`operator_fee` published but unused.** APR is net of it (0–30% across the
  sample), ours is gross. A known, correctable gap in magnitude.
