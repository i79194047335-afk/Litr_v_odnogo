## Why this is a separate hypothesis, and a disclosure about blindness

H-005 was pre-registered and measured. Its result is now what needs attacking,
and it cannot be attacked as H-005 because **I already know the outcome** —
running the protocol on H-005 would give the falsifier a claim whose answer sits
in `H-005.result.md`, committed to `main`.

So the claim below is the *result*, stated as a fresh falsifiable proposition.
The falsifier gets the claim, its numbers and their sources, and does not get
`H-005.result.md`.

**Disclosure, because the protocol's guarantee is mechanical and this run
partly loses it:** in a normal run Claude writes its verdict blind, before
unsealing. Here I have already done the measurement, so my verdict is not blind
in that sense. What remains intact is the direction that matters more for this
project's failure mode — the falsifier attacks numbers it has not seen me
defend, and the number-provenance audit (`CONSENSUS_PROTOCOL.md`) runs against
figures I produced today and have already written into `docs/MINING.md`. Treat a
"CONFIRMED" here as weaker than one from a fully blind round.

## Claim

**A.** On the day-pair 31 Jul → 1 Aug 2026, restricted to accounts that cleared
50 fills on **every** day 29 Jul – 2 Aug, daily realised-PnL rank correlation
stands above its own permutation null band on all three markets measured
(0, 2, 24) — and this is not an artefact of the restriction, because the same
restriction leaves the other three adjacent pairs inside the null band or
negative.

**B.** On market 0 the effect is a *rank* effect and not a *magnitude* effect:
Spearman +0.581 against Pearson +0.023 over the same 143 accounts and the same
two days. Therefore the observation cannot size a position, only order accounts.

**C.** Daily-PnL ranking is rejected as a selection mechanism for this track.
Of four adjacent pairs on complete days, three sit in the null band on every
market; the fourth is the pair above.

## What would falsify each part

- **A** falls if the core restriction can be shown to manufacture correlation —
  e.g. if intersecting five days selects on the very quantity being correlated,
  or if the permutation null is built on a different population than the
  statistic.
- **B** falls if the Spearman/Pearson gap is an artefact of outliers or of
  comparing a robust statistic to a non-robust one on a heavy-tailed variable.
  Note this is the part I consider weakest.
- **C** falls if a pair currently called "noise" is above its null under a
  defensible alternative threshold, or if `min_fills=50` is doing the work.

## Known limits, stated up front

- **One pair out of four.** Twelve checks at a 5% false-positive rate expect 0.6
  false alarms; three were seen, all on one pair. Whether that concentration is
  itself unlikely **cannot be computed here**: the three markets share accounts,
  so they are not independent tests. No p-value is claimed for the synchrony.
- **Five complete days.** 3 Aug excluded everywhere (18.4 h of 24).
- **No order-book data** exists for these days; book collection began 3 Aug.
- **The mechanism is unknown.** H-005 proposed a compositional one and its own
  pre-registered criterion refuted it. Nothing is offered in its place.
- **Not tradeable, and no decision rests on this.** It is recorded so it is
  neither rediscovered nor overclaimed.

## Specific questions for the falsifier

1. The number-provenance audit, per `CONSENSUS_PROTOCOL.md`: for every figure
   above, is the named source sufficient, and is anything a ghost?
2. Does intersecting five days to build the "core" induce the correlation it is
   used to test? Selection on activity is selection on *something*; is it
   selection on PnL rank?
3. Is Spearman +0.581 with Pearson +0.023 a real rank/magnitude split, or the
   expected behaviour of two different statistics on a heavy-tailed variable
   with a handful of large accounts?
4. Is the permutation null in `pnl_persistence` the right null for the
   core-restricted case, given the core was chosen using all five days
   including both days of the pair?
