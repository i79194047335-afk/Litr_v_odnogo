# H-004 — Claude's verdict (written blind, before unseal)

VERDICT: A CONFIRMED. B CONFIRMED BUT WEAKER THAN ITS p-VALUE SUGGESTS —
THE SAMPLE IS SELECTED, AND ONE OF ITS TWO HEADLINE FIGURES IS DOUBLE-COUNTED.

## What holds

**A.** Not really a hypothesis so much as a lookup that either matches or does
not. All 319 enumerated pools carry type 2 or 3; the two type-3 entries are
named LLP and XLP and carry the zero L1 address. The reverse direction was
checked independently before this claim was written: `accountsByL1Address` on
two operator addresses returned exactly one type-2 account each, which is the counter-query `WORKFLOW.md` §4a demands and not another
one-directional observation. H-002's "type 3 = system account, 100% maker" is
not contradicted — it is explained. The venue's market maker is itself a
public pool.

**B, in direction.** Sign agrees on 9 of 11, and — the check that matters —
the naive strategy of always predicting "positive" would score only 6 of 11,
because 55% of the traded sample has positive APR. So the coin-flip null is
not obviously too generous, and the result is not an artifact of most pools
being profitable. Among all 140 pools with `status=1` only 39% have positive APR; across all
319 the base rate is lower still. An always-positive guess does not beat 9/11
under any of these readings.

Spearman +0.52 over 11, +0.72 over the 9 with real activity, is consistent
with the sign result rather than an independent confirmation of it.

**Stronger than the claim asserts, and found while writing this verdict.**
The claim deliberately restricts itself to sign and order because APR and a
5-day gross figure are not commensurable. But normalising our PnL by the
pool's published `total_asset_value` — a field the reconstruction never used —
puts the two on comparable footing, and the magnitudes line up:

| pool | our 5d return on TVL | published APR |
|---|---|---|
| LLP | +0.029% | +11.04% |
| Edge & Hedge | +0.328% | +12.73% |
| Syzygy | +7.859% | +56.50% |
| DSC - L | +5.862% | +322.66% |
| PHP Trading Desk | −1.875% | −7.72% |
| Alleged Alpha | −0.395% | −12.07% |

Five days at +0.029% annualises to roughly +2%, against a published 11%; at
+7.86% it annualises to far more than 56%. So these do not match numerically —
but they are the right sign and the right order of magnitude across four
decades of pool size, which sign agreement alone would not have shown. The
claim understates what the data supports.

## What does not hold, or holds more weakly than stated

**The 9/9 figure is not a second, stronger result.** It is the same 11
observations with the two smallest removed, and those two are exactly the
disagreements. Reporting "9 of 11, P = 0.033" and then "9 of 9, P = 0.002" as
if the second strengthens the first is close to double-counting: the filter is
defensible on its own terms (`fills >= 20` is used throughout the track and
predates this run), but applying it and then quoting the improved p-value is a
choice made after seeing which pools disagreed. The honest headline is 9/11,
P = 0.033. The 9/9 line belongs in the notes as a robustness check, not in the
claim.

**The claim calls 140 pools "active" and I cannot support that.** `status=1`
was read as active when writing the hypothesis. It is not: all 11 pools that
actually trade in our tape carry `status=0`, including LLP with $76.6M of
assets and Edge & Hedge with $2.06M. Nor does the field separate cleanly on
holdings — 103 of 179 `status=0` pools report TVL above zero, and so do 51 of
140 `status=1`. **The meaning of `status` is not established, and by the
project's own rule it must be recorded as unknown rather than guessed.** Every
"140 active" in the claim should read "140 with status=1", and the count of
pools that are live in any economic sense is not known.

**The sample is selected, and the claim does not say so.** Eleven of 319 pools
— 3% — appear in our tape, and they are not a random 3%. They are the pools
trading the four most liquid markets we happen to collect. The visible set is
plainly the large end: LLP holds $76.6M and Edge & Hedge $2.06M, while the
smallest visible pools hold ~$900. Anything that makes a pool visible to us
could correlate with whether its published APR is honest or its strategy
legible. This does not undermine the direction of the result, but it forbids
generalising from "our reconstruction agrees for these 11" to "our
reconstruction agrees for pools" — the defensible reading is that it agrees
for *large, actively trading* pools.

**n = 11 with P = 0.033 is one experiment, not a body of evidence.** The
project has an explicit precedent here: range bars were run five times on one
unvalidated scale and counted as five experiments. This is one comparison. It
should be re-run when more days accumulate, and the pass criterion for that
re-run should be written before it.

**One number in the claim is stale by a few hours.** Edge & Hedge is quoted at
+7,461.94 over 3,085 fills; an earlier run of the same tool over fewer hours of
2026-08-02 gave +6,774.66 over 3,066. Both are correct for their windows, but a
figure that moves while the tape is being appended to needs its window stated,
and the claim states only the day range.

## Ghosts found

None, and deliberately so. `pool_validation.py` (14 tests) was written before
this hypothesis was submitted, precisely because H-003 was returned UNTESTABLE
twice for numbers computed in scratch scripts. Every figure in the table is
reproducible with one command. The endpoint is unauthenticated, so the
falsifier can re-derive the enumeration independently.

The one number not from a tested tool is "index=0 returns an empty list;
pagination runs downward", which is a direct observation of the API and
re-checkable with `curl`.

## What would change the verdict

- More days, and pools accumulating enough fills that the sample is not 11.
  If agreement holds at n = 25+ the result stops being one experiment.
- A pool whose published APR is strongly positive while our reconstruction is
  strongly negative **on a large fill count** would be a genuine refutation.
  Dragon and EAGLE are not that: 4 and 2 fills.
- Establishing what `status` means, by counter-query if the venue offers one.
  It is currently unknown, and the hypothesis leaned on a wrong reading of it.
- `operator_fee` is published and was not used. Published APR is net of it;
  ours is gross. For a pool charging 30%, that is a known, correctable gap in
  magnitude — irrelevant to sign, but it means the two series could be brought
  closer than "not commensurable" implies.

## Consequence for the track

This is the first external referent the reconstruction has had, and it points
the right way. It does not license treating reconstructed PnL as validated in
magnitude — nothing here tests magnitude at all.

Its more durable value may be the labelled accounts rather than the check:
11 accounts with a name, a declared strategy and an outside performance
figure, against which behaviour can be read. `Edge & Hedge (L/S Factors)` at
100% maker across four markets is the hedge construction `MINING.md` §4.2
warned about, now available by name instead of hypothetically.
