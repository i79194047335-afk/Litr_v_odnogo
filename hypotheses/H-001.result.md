### Where they diverged

**1. The verdict label — definitional, not factual.** The two agree on every
fact and disagree on how to label a *compound* claim. H-001 has two limbs:
(a) "widening was tested and made results worse" — a factual claim about the
record, which is false → REFUTED; (b) "2 is optimal" — no metric, split, or cost
assumption defined → UNTESTABLE. Claude labelled on limb (a), DeepSeek on limb
(b). Both are defensible.

> **Protocol lesson: the schema forces one label onto a compound hypothesis.**
> H-files must state ONE atomic claim. Split compound claims into H-001a /
> H-001b before running. This is a real defect found by the shakedown.

**2. DeepSeek misattributed a citation.** It cited `AUDIT_2026-07-10.md` "item
S2" as stating that `swing_confirm_bars` was never swept. S2 is a real item with
that real ID — but it is about **`acceptance_bars`**, and says nothing about
`swing_confirm_bars`. Real document, real anchor, wrong content: a
plausible-looking citation that does not support the claim it was cited for —
the project's own signature failure mode, produced by the falsifier itself.

> **Protocol lesson: every citation in the falsifier's output must be opened and
> checked.** It reached the right conclusion via a fabricated support. Unchecked,
> that citation would have become the next ghost.

**3. DeepSeek's kill-shot would have burned the holdout.** It proposed sweeping N
"on the untouched holdout days (2026-07-07 through 2026-07-09)". Per
`docs/PASS_FAIL_CRITERION.md` the holdout is "Jul 7 onward — touched ONCE, at
the end; that number is the number". Running a **parameter sweep** on it destroys
the project's only clean evaluation set. It also specified `costs zero`, which is
how a fake optimum is manufactured. The closed day range `07-07..07-09` is also
asserted, not stated in the doc.

> **Protocol lesson: the falsifier proposes kill-shots but must not choose the
> evaluation split.** That is a pass/fail decision, already on the
> do-not-delegate list. Kill-shots come back as *candidate* measurements.

**4. Each caught something the other missed.**

Claude only:
- `N = 1` exists (`SwingTracker` validates `N >= 1`) — 2 was never compared
  *downward*, only allegedly upward.
- The three prose sites most plausibly descend from **one** unrecorded
  observation; one ghost cited three times reads like corroboration.
- `swing_confirm_bars` × `range_size` is a 2-D scale interaction, and the frozen
  axis (`range_size=15.3`) is itself an uncalibrated forum heuristic.
- A cheaper kill-shot: search the repo/history for the artifact first (seconds,
  no backtest).

DeepSeek only:
- A bug or a single outlier day as the cause of the alleged worsening.
- Interaction with `trailing` not isolated.
- The sharpest phrasing of the load-bearing assumption: *"the outcome of that
  test was recorded and is trustworthy despite the absence of any numeric
  record."*

### What round 2 added that round 1 did not have

1. **The sweep as designed answers a question about a regime we no longer use.**
   Round 1 (Claude) flagged `swing_confirm_bars` × `range_size` as a 2-D
   interaction with an uncalibrated frozen axis. Round 2 supplied the part that
   changes the design: the project has since moved to **rolling daily
   recalibration** (AUDIT A4, `ROLLING_CALIBRATION_2026-07-10.md`), so a sweep
   at the constant `range_size=15.3` measures optimality under the fixed-size
   regime, not the live-capable one. The queued measurement below is corrected
   accordingly. This is the clearest single gain from the second round.
2. **Claude's own kill-shot cited a suspended document.** `H-001.claude.md:110`
   proposes sweeping "on the fixed day split from `docs/PASS_FAIL_CRITERION.md`"
   — the file whose own banner says its split is invalid and must not be used
   that way. The later resolution (below) does not make that error, but the
   round-1 verdict contained it, and round 2 found it. A verdict that flags a
   ghost while resting on a suspended source is the failure mode in miniature.
3. **"Costs as configured, not zero" is imprecise.** Round 2 called it
   untraceable; that part is wrong — `config.yaml:37-39` configures
   `fee_maker_bps: 0`, `fee_taker_bps: 0`, `slippage_ticks: 1`. But its
   underlying point lands: the configured fees *are* zero, so "as configured
   rather than zero" reads as a stronger cost assumption than it is. Only
   slippage is non-zero. Restated precisely below.
4. **Process point, fair:** the artifact search is reported as a conclusion
   without its raw output. Cheap to fix and worth fixing — a proof of a negative
   that nobody can re-read is one restatement away from being the next ghost.

### What round 2 got wrong — and the protocol defect it exposes

**DeepSeek proposed burning the holdout again, twice.** Both `DISPUTED → SETTLE`
and `NEW-KILL-SHOT` call for sweeping N = 1..5 on "the untouched holdout days
2026-07-07 through 2026-07-09", with `costs zero`. Its `MISSED` item 2 goes
further and argues the holdout "could be used without waiting for a new split
decision" — contradicting the banner it had itself just cited correctly.

This is the same error as round 1, and the round-2 system prompt **named that
exact prior failure and forbade it** (rule 4: do not choose the evaluation
split, with the holdout incident given as the reason). It did it anyway.

> **Protocol lesson: the prohibition cannot live in the prompt.** Telling the
> falsifier not to choose the evaluation split does not stop it, even when the
> instruction cites its own previous instance of doing so. The gate has to be
> procedural: every kill-shot returns as a *candidate*, and the split, the cost
> model and the thresholds are read off `PASS_FAIL_CRITERION.md` and Ivan's
> decision, never off the falsifier's proposal. Rounds 1 and 2 are now two
> independent confirmations of this, from a model that had been told.

Also unchanged from round 1: it re-argues REFUTED vs UNTESTABLE. That is the
compound-hypothesis defect already recorded above, not new information.

### Net assessment of the second round

Coverage gain is real and it comes from **non-overlap, not agreement** — one
finding that changes the measurement design (the rolling-calibration regime),
one real defect in Claude's verdict (the suspended-split citation), one useful
sharpening (the cost phrasing), one fair process point. Against that: one
prohibited kill-shot repeated twice, and one wrong claim about traceability.

Cost: 1 API call, `prompt_chars=167302`, `finish_reason=stop`. The verification
tax is the real cost — four citations and two disputed claims had to be opened
by hand, and one of the six findings was wrong in a way that reads as confident.
Round 2 is worth running, and worth never taking at face value.
