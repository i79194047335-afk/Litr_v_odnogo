# H-007: rank persistence grows over the collection window

Status: open
Proposed: 2026-08-06
Proposer: Claude

## What changed since H-005/H-006

Those were decided on five days. There are now eight, and the picture is
different enough that the earlier verdict — "daily-PnL ranking is rejected as a
selection mechanism" — no longer describes the data.

Pairs built only from complete days (29 Jul excluded: 5.7 h, first day of
collection), `min_fills=50`, full population:

| 31 Jul→1 Aug | +0.4229 | +0.3176 | +0.3877 | 3 |
| 1→2 Aug | −0.0326 | −0.1551 | +0.2231 | 1 |
| 2→3 Aug | −0.0367 | +0.1709 | +0.1387 | 1 |
| 3→4 Aug | +0.0445 | +0.2873 | +0.4349 | 2 |

**10 of 18 checks above their own null band. Pure noise predicts 0.9.**

The `0804→0805` pair also survives the attack that `0731→0801` survived —
core selected from days outside the pair (1, 2, 3 Aug): m0 +0.2828 (p95 +0.1298),
m2 +0.6016 (p95 +0.1943), m24 +0.7389 (p95 +0.2181), 3 of 3. Unlike the July
pair, its Pearson does not collapse when the three largest |PnL| accounts are
dropped (m2 +0.404 → +0.653, m24 +0.543 → +0.493), so this one is not a
heavy-tail artefact.

## Claim

**A.** Day-over-day rank correlation of realised PnL is **not stationary** over
29 Jul – 5 Aug. It trends upward: the earliest complete pairs are negative or in
noise, the latest three give 1, 2 and 3 markets above their null bands.

**B.** The trend is **not explained by data volume**. Fills and account counts on
m24 are non-monotonic across the window (29 Jul 13 997 fills / 527 accounts;
31 Jul 44 527 / 857; 2 Aug 30 233 / 686; 4 Aug 28 001 / 703; 5 Aug 40 322 / 812)
while the correlation trend is monotonic in the later half.

**C.** Therefore the H-005/H-006 verdict "ranking rejected" is **scoped to its
sample, not general**. It is correct for the first pairs and does not describe
the last three.

## What this hypothesis does NOT claim

- That ranking works as a selection mechanism. Ten of eighteen is far from
  reliable, and no pair is above noise on all three markets except two.
- That the cause is known. Two candidate mechanisms are named below and this
  hypothesis does not choose between them — that is the point of the criterion.
- Anything tradeable.

## Discriminating criterion, written BEFORE the measurement

The two live explanations predict opposite things:

- **Market explanation:** something about the venue changed over the window
  (participants settling, a regime, listings). Prediction: the trend continues
  or holds on pairs from 5–8 Aug, and is visible in markets we did *not* use to
  find it — markets 1 (BTC) is collected but was excluded from every run so far.
- **Instrument explanation:** the trend is an artefact of the collection
  maturing — early days lost fills, later days did not, and a more complete tape
  mechanically produces more stable PnL estimates. Prediction: per-day fill
  counts or gap statistics correlate with that day's pair correlation, and
  market 1 shows **no** trend because whatever changed is per-market.

**Thresholds:**
- Market explanation supported if m1 (never used to derive this) shows the same
  upward trend, ρ rising across the same pairs.
- Instrument explanation supported if a completeness metric per day (fills/hour
  variance, or gaps in the tape) tracks the correlation with |ρ_spearman| > 0.6
  across the 6 pairs.
- If neither: unresolved, and the honest answer is that eight days is too short.

**Holdout, declared now:** market 1 has not been touched in any of this. It is
the out-of-sample check and will be run **once**, after the criterion above is
committed.

**Holdout status 2026-08-06: BLOCKED, not run, not silently skipped.** Market 1
carries ~1.01M tape records per day against m24's 44.5k — 23× larger. Two such
days held at once exceeds the box: every attempt died with `rc=137` (OOM),
confirmed in the kernel log (`Out of memory: Killed process … anon-rss:3.1 GB`)
on a 3.9 GB machine already running the tape and book collectors.

One pair did complete before memory filled, and it is recorded here so the
holdout is not re-derived from scratch: `0730→0731`, ρ **−0.1068**, null p95
+0.0530, 892 accounts present both days — **within noise**, matching m0/m2/m24
on that same pair (0 of 3). One pair is not the holdout; it is one data point
that happens to agree.

**A defect of my own tooling surfaced here and is worth recording:** the first
holdout script reported OOM-killed runs as `шум` (noise), because it inferred
the verdict from the absence of an "above noise" line rather than from the exit
code. A tool that reports failure as a result is exactly what `WORKFLOW.md` §2.3
forbids. Fixed to distinguish `FAILED rc=…` from a real measurement before any
number here was read.

**Why chunking is not the easy fix.** `in_time_order` (`pnl_probe.py`) returns a
list, and must: the collector appends WS frames as they arrive and fills are
newest-first *within* each frame, so the tape is genuinely out of order.
Reconstruction scores 41.8% unsorted against 99.99% sorted — the sort is a
correctness requirement, not an oversight, and it needs the whole day resident.

So the options are, in order of cost: run the holdout on a box with more memory;
or pause the collectors for the ~30 minutes it takes (**rejected by me, not
Ivan's call to make lightly** — the tape is the one asset here that cannot be
re-created, and a gap in it is permanent); or accept the holdout as pending.

Recorded as pending. **A, B and C stand without out-of-sample confirmation**,
which is a stated limit, not an oversight. This is the kind of decision
`CLAUDE.md` §3 reserves for Ivan: it trades a permanent hole in the tape against
an earlier answer on a hypothesis that nothing currently depends on.
