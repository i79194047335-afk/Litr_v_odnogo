# H-003 — Claude's verdict (written blind, before unseal)

VERDICT: A CONFIRMED, B CONFIRMED BUT UNDERSTATED — AND THE CLAIM AS WRITTEN
HIDES ITS OWN WEAKEST TEST

Claim A holds. Claim B holds, but two of its three numbers were produced by a
comparison I set up badly, and the honest version of B is stronger and worse
for the track than what I wrote.

## What holds

**A — the arithmetic.** The position book-keeping predicts the venue's own next
reported state on 99.956%–99.995% of checks, across 4 markets and 2 days,
988,998 checks on the largest single slice. The oracle is external: the venue
computed those figures on its own books, and the check is a forward prediction,
not a self-consistency test. The residue is sparse and unstructured — 5 misses
on 5 distinct accounts on market 24 / 20260731, none repeating, none flagged
`position_sign_changed`. A broken accounting rule would concentrate on the
accounts that exercise it.

The 41.85%-versus-99.99% gap is the load-bearing detail here, and it cuts
*for* A: the arithmetic was never in question, only the read order. That is
also the strongest available evidence that this class of error masquerades as
a research finding.

**Persistence of ids** is settled independently: 3368/3376 identical on
`account_type` and `l1_address`, zero identity changes, zero vanished, zero
unreachable. Ranking by account id is not undermined by id reuse over this
horizon.

**B's direction.** Day-to-day top-20 overlap is low, and on markets 24 and 2 it
does not clear a random-draw baseline. That much survives.

## What does not hold

**B's numbers for markets 24 and 2 are contaminated by a selection artifact I
introduced.** `min_fills=20` is applied per day, so day 2's top-20 is drawn
from a different eligible population than day 1's. An account can leave the
top-20 by trading less, not by earning less. Re-running with both rankings
restricted to accounts eligible on *both* days:

| market | overlap as claimed | overlap within common set |
|---|---|---|
| 24 | 4/20 | **8/20** |
| 2 | 5/20 | 5/20 |
| 0 | 8/20 | 9/20 |

So "4/20 on market 24" is not a measurement of rank instability; it is partly a
measurement of turnover in who shows up. The claim should never have compared
two differently-filtered populations. Market 2 is unchanged, market 0 barely
moves — but the headline number for market 24 doubles, and it was the number I
led with.

**B understates the result.** The sharper test is not overlap of a top-20 but
whether the *sign* of an account's PnL repeats. Among accounts eligible on both
days:

- market 24: 71/147 agree = **48.3%**
- market 2: 68/152 agree = **44.7%**
- market 0: 201/403 agree = **49.9%**

A coin flip is 50%. Two of three sit slightly below it. Whether an account made
or lost money yesterday carries no information about today, on this horizon and
this metric. That is a stronger negative than "top-20 overlap is within noise",
and I did not put it in the claim.

## Ghosts found

**One in my own claim, by the project's definition.** The baseline p95 values
(5 / 5 / 3) come from a 2000-draw simulation with `seed 7` that exists only in
a scratch script — not in the repository, not in a tool, not reproducible from
anything committed. Every other number in the table traces to a committed tool
with tests. These three do not. By CLAUDE.md §2 they are ghosts until the
simulation is a registered tool with a test, and B's verdict leans on them.

Not ghosts, but stated limits that any use of these numbers inherits:

- All PnL is **gross**. `maker_fee`/`taker_fee` units are undocumented and
  unmeasured; the median implied maker fee under the only obvious guess is not
  negligible against these edges, so net ranking may differ from gross.
- **Realised only.** Open positions are unmarked, and 422 of 857 accounts end
  market 24 / 20260731 holding something. An account's day is not summarised by
  its realised figure alone.
- The totals do not net to zero (−11,679 then +41,378), because the venue's own
  positions do not net either (+91,057). Inventory, not bias — the sign flips
  between days — but it means no total here can be sanity-checked against zero.

## What would change the verdict

- **For B specifically:** more than two days. Two consecutive days is one
  comparison, not a measurement of persistence, and 2026-07-28..08-02 is on
  disk. If sign agreement stays at ~50% over five days and across markets,
  B stops being "on this horizon" and becomes a property.
- The permutation baseline promoted to a tested tool, or B's noise comparison
  restated without it.
- A longer aggregation window (whole period rather than per day) before
  ranking. Per-day PnL of a market maker is close to noise by construction;
  the track's question was never "who won today".
- Fee units established. If fees are of the same order as these edges, the
  entire ranking is provisional regardless of what B says about stability.

## Consequence for the track, stated plainly

A is what MINING.md step 3 asked for, and it passes: the reconstruction works.
B says the naive use of it — rank by daily PnL, copy the top — does not select
anything that persists. Those are compatible, and B is not a reason to close
the track. It is a reason to reject per-day PnL ranking as the selection
mechanism and to look at longer windows and at behaviour rather than outcome.
