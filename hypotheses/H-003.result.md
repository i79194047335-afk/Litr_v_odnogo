## What replaces it — and what is now unexplained

**The one thing that must not be written yet is "profitable participants
persist".** The signal is not distributed across day-pairs; it sits entirely on
`0731 → 0801`, and it appears there on **three independent markets at once**.
Raising `min_fills` to 100 and 200 to equalise eligibility does not remove it —
ρ rises to +0.53 / +0.25 / +0.55 — so it is not the selection artifact that
broke the original claim.

Synchronicity across three markets points at a property of **those two days**,
not of the participants. Candidate: a market-wide regime (both days quieter —
market 0 carried 131k fills on 0801 against 212k on 0731) in which the same
directional exposure paid twice. Under that reading the correlation measures
inventory that survived the day boundary, not skill.

Both files span a full 24h, so this is not a truncation artifact.

Adjacent pairs run mildly *negative* (−0.07, −0.16, −0.09), which no
skill-based story explains either.

**Written before the next run, per `MINING.md` §6:** if per-day ρ on
`0801 → 0802` and beyond returns to the null band on all three markets, the
0731→0801 result is a two-day regime and per-day PnL ranking stays rejected as
a selection mechanism. If ρ stays above the null on further pairs, persistence
is real and the horizon question reopens.

## Post-close addendum, 2026-08-02: the pre-registered criterion, and an external anchor

### The criterion was run and its answer is mixed

Written before the run: *"if per-day ρ on `0801 → 0802` and beyond returns to
the null band **on all three markets**, the 0731→0801 result is a two-day
regime."*

| market | 0731→0801 | 0801→0802 | null p95 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | +0.355 | **+0.116** | +0.091 | above noise |
| 2 | +0.297 | **+0.036** | +0.152 | within noise |
| 24 | +0.415 | **+0.578** | +0.148 | above noise |

It did **not** return to the null band on all three. The "two-day regime"
explanation is therefore rejected as stated: market 24 strengthened rather
than decayed, and sign agreement there rose to 71.7%.

But this does not establish persistence either. The three markets now disagree
with each other, which no single story covers: a participant property should
not be strong on HYPE, marginal on ETH and absent on SOL.

**Limitation, stated rather than ignored:** the 0802 file covers **16.6 hours,
not 24** — collection is still running. The pair 0801→0802 is not comparable
like-for-like with the others. A partial day truncates each account's activity,
which shifts eligibility and can inflate or deflate ρ. This pair must be re-run
once 0802 closes before anything is concluded from it.

**Next criterion, written before that run:** re-run 0801→0802 on the closed
file. If market 24 stays above +0.4 with the full day, the effect is not an
artifact of partial coverage and the horizon question is genuinely open. If it
collapses toward the others, the whole 0731→0802 sequence is coverage noise.

### External validation of the reconstruction — the first in this track

Until now realised PnL was checked against nothing: the venue publishes no PnL
figure, so both branches of any internal cross-check could be wrong together.
Lighter's Public Pools showcase supplies an independent number.

`GET /api/v1/publicPoolsMetadata` (no auth) enumerates **319 pools, 140
active**, each carrying `account_index`, `name`, `annual_percentage_yield` and
`sharpe_ratio`. Eleven of them traded on our 4 markets during the 5 collected
days.

**Sign of the showcase APR versus sign of our reconstructed PnL: 9 of 11 agree
(P = 0.033 against a coin flip). Spearman between showcase APR and our 5-day
PnL: +0.52.**

Both disagreements are `EAGLE` (2 fills) and `Dragon` (4 fills) — sample sizes
at which five days says nothing about an annual figure.

This is weak evidence in the sense that the quantities are not commensurable
(annual vs 5-day, after operator fee vs gross, ~90 markets vs 4) and it tests
only sign and order. It is nonetheless the first time a number produced by this
code has been checked against a number produced by someone else.
